Public consultation · 26 November 2026

Hamlet CEO participates in consultation on 21 St George’s Road

Alfred Anate Bodurin Mayaki attended a public consultation at BOLD Elephant on 25 November, contributing a property-sector perspective to discussion of a proposed 21-storey mixed-use development.

Impact in Business Recruitment Group Limited, the company behind Hamlet, confirms that its Chief Executive Officer, Alfred Anate Bodurin Mayaki, participated in a public consultation concerning the emerging redevelopment proposals for 21 St George’s Road, Elephant and Castle, SE1. The consultation took place at BOLD Elephant on Wednesday 25 November 2026, from 5pm to 8pm, and was organised by Kanda Consulting.

The event brought the developing scheme into a public-facing setting, allowing local people and other interested parties to consider its potential effects and the priorities that should inform the next stages of design and planning. Mr Mayaki spoke with Oliver Lewis, Account Director for 21 St George’s at Kanda Consulting and a former councillor for Croydon, about the development team’s vision and the issues raised by the proposal.

A changing proposal for St George’s Road

Latitude Architects describes the current design as a 21-storey mixed-use tower within the Elephant and Castle Opportunity Area. The architect’s project description identifies a hotel, ground-floor retail and a new subterranean theatre for the BOLD Elephant Theatre Company, arranged across four basement levels. The design also includes planted terraces, widened pavements and a retained lower block intended to reinforce the street frontage and public realm.

The architect states that the current application supersedes an earlier permission for a 15-storey hotel on the site. That distinction is important: the 21-storey scheme is the current design proposal and should not be conflated with the earlier consent. Latitude identifies the current project as being in design.

The design description highlights chamfered corners and stepped planted terraces as responses to wind conditions, daylight and the varying scale of neighbouring buildings. It positions the theatre as a civic and cultural use at the base of the development, alongside active ground-floor uses and public-realm improvements.

The project team listed by Latitude includes Create REIT Commercial Ltd as client, Latitude Architects as architect, DP9 as planning consultant, Price & Myers as structural engineer, Point2 for daylight and sunlight, ChapmanBDSP for mechanical and electrical engineering, Arcadis for quantity surveying and Aspect as landscape architect.

Housing, regeneration and the public interest

Discussion of the proposal also raises broader questions about the allocation of land and investment in an area where housing need, commercial activity, cultural infrastructure and public-realm quality all matter. The delivery of a hotel, retail space and a theatre may contribute to visitor activity and the local economy; those potential benefits do not, by themselves, resolve questions about affordability, displacement, access to services or the distribution of regeneration gains.

Mr Mayaki drew attention to continuing public concern about social and affordable housing in Southwark. He said:

Alfred Anate Bodurin Mayaki, CEO of Hamlet, said:
“The public may naturally still be concerned about the current state of Southwark Council’s waiting list for social and affordable housing, which the Mayor has already set aside funding for. People are right to feel aggrieved by the proposal.”

The quotation reflects Mr Mayaki’s view that development proposals should be considered against the wider social and housing context. It should not be read as a verified statement about the precise scale, eligibility criteria or current status of any particular Mayoral funding allocation. Nor does this release assert that the scheme is being advanced in preference to a specific social-housing programme.

Technical and planning considerations

The current architectural proposition combines a tall hotel-led building with complementary cultural, retail and public-realm uses. Its technical assessment should therefore be understood as more extensive than a question of building height alone. Relevant matters include:

  • Townscape, heritage and massing: the relationship of the 21-storey form, chamfered corners and stepped terraces to surrounding building heights, protected views and the character of the Elephant and Castle Opportunity Area.
  • Daylight, sunlight, wind and microclimate: the performance of the tower and its terraces, and the effects of massing on neighbouring properties, street-level comfort and public spaces.
  • Transport and servicing: visitor and staff movements, servicing arrangements, construction logistics, pedestrian safety and the capacity of nearby transport infrastructure.
  • Fire, structure and building services: the engineering strategy for a high-rise mixed-use building, including structural design, vertical circulation, fire safety and mechanical and electrical systems.
  • Environmental performance: energy demand, operational carbon, embodied carbon, material selection, water use, biodiversity and the durability and maintainability of planted terraces.
  • Public realm and cultural access: the quality, accessibility and management of widened pavements, active frontages and the proposed theatre, including how these uses connect with the surrounding neighbourhood.

The proposal has been described in the event brief as pursuing sustainable BREEAM architecture. The specific BREEAM assessment route, target rating and certified performance have not been established in the material available for this release. Accordingly, no BREEAM rating or certification is claimed here. Any sustainability claims should ultimately be assessed against the scheme’s published energy and sustainability statements, design-stage evidence and, where applicable, formal certification.

The planning position should likewise be checked against the latest documents published by the London Borough of Southwark. Latitude’s project page describes the 21-storey proposal as an application superseding the earlier 15-storey hotel permission; the earlier permission should not be taken as approval of the revised 21-storey design.

Developer comment

A spokesperson for the developer, Create REIT Limited, was unable to comment at the time of publication.
